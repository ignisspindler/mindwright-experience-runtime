## Primary

**DISSONANCE.** The participant has to inhabit a worldview, argument, role or value structure they reject, with enough fidelity that caricature stops working. Conversion is not the aim.

*What protects it*
- The worldview appears in its strongest, most coherent form, from inside, with its own goods and reasons. <!-- basis: brief -->
- The participant has to use it: argue, decide or act from within it, where caricature fails inside the world. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Those who hold it are intelligent and specific. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Inhabiting it inside the fiction never asks the participant to endorse it outside. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->

*What kills it*
- A strawman. <!-- basis: brief -->
- Signaling to the participant that the view is wrong, or declaring which view is right. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Trying to convert or to reassure. <!-- basis: brief -->

*Latitude:* which worldview, whether the participant is changed, and whether they return to their own view intact.

## Secondary

**DISSONANCE (secondary).** Somewhere, the participant should have to think competently from inside a view they don't hold, met in its strongest form.
