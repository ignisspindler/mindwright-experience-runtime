## Primary

**SEDUCTION.** Something is influencing the participant, and they keep wanting it anyway. The pull may come from charisma, belonging, recognition, certainty, status, ideology, desire or authority. It need not be sexual, and the boundaries govern any desire.

*What protects it*
- The pull is real: it offers something the participant would actually want, and gives some of it. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- The influence is recognizable, and the wanting continues anyway. <!-- basis: brief -->
- Refusal remains genuinely possible, and has costs. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Knowing they are being courted doesn't neutralize wanting; the participant's awareness of the frame is material. <!-- basis: author directive -->

*What kills it*
- A lure so plainly sinister that no one would want it. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Moralizing about the pull, or rescuing the participant from it. <!-- basis: brief -->
- Coercion taking the place of attraction. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->

*Latitude:* what attracts, whether the participant yields, whether yielding is wrong, and whether the work ever judges them.

## Secondary

**SEDUCTION (secondary).** Something in the work should attract the participant with a pull they can recognize as influence and still want.
