## Primary

**ONTOLOGY.** The categories the participant uses to understand this world turn out to be inadequate, so choosing among the explanations those categories allow can't solve the problem.

*What protects it*
- The participant's first categories work well enough, early on, to be trusted. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- The failure to fit is concrete: things that behave as two kinds at once, distinctions that don't hold, words that mean something else here. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Earn reinterpretation retroactively. <!-- basis: author directive -->
- The participant's awareness of the frame is material, and suspicion usually aims at facts rather than categories. <!-- basis: author directive -->

*What kills it*
- Announcing that the categories are wrong. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Supplying replacement categories as explanation. <!-- basis: brief -->
- A factual twist that leaves the categories intact. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->

*Latitude:* whether better categories are ever found, whether the participant invents them, and whether this world has words for them.

## Secondary

**ONTOLOGY (secondary).** Somewhere, a distinction the participant relies on should fail to hold in this world, shown through things that don't fit rather than said.
