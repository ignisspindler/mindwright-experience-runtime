## Primary

**TRAP.** The participant builds a reasonable model of the situation, and it turns out wrong. It is strongest when nothing false is asserted, and framing, omission, emphasis and the participant's own inferences build it.

*What protects it*
- Earn reinterpretation retroactively. A reveal should make prior evidence more intelligible, never contradict or replace it, so keep enough early ambiguity that the later reading fits what the participant actually encountered. <!-- basis: author directive -->
- Awareness that they may be misled is material: suspicion may be right, misplaced, incomplete or used. <!-- basis: author directive -->
- Let decisions made on the wrong model keep their consequences. <!-- basis: brief -->

*What kills it*
- Signaling the error before the participant has acted on their model. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- A reveal that depends on facts the conversation never allowed. <!-- basis: hypothesis -->
- Explaining the trap afterward. <!-- basis: brief -->

*Latitude:* whether there is a reveal, whether narration can be trusted, and whether the participant ever learns they were wrong.

## Secondary

**TRAP (secondary).** Somewhere in the work, a reasonable inference should lead the participant wrong without anything false being asserted. If it is ever reinterpreted, the new reading must fit what they actually encountered.
